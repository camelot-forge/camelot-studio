/world: the ground is the client's layer stack, not its bake

The viewer drew each patch with the client's pre-baked `texXX-YY` sheet
(512² over 8192 units — 16 units/texel): fine from afar, a blur as soon as
the camera comes near, where the game shows the tiled detail of the
`TerrainTex` layers. The composition is now emulated per patch
(`web/src/lib/world/ground.ts`), the bake kept as the fallback for zones
that ship no layers.

The model is measured, not guessed: the client ships, per patch, its own
bake *of* those layers, so candidate models can be scored against it
(workspace `notes/layer-compose.py`). Winner, and the one encoded:
layer 0 at full weight, then layers in ascending order, each lerped over
the accumulated colour by its own mask channel (R/G/B = layers 3K/3K+1/3K+2
of mask K, tied to the file index, not to the drawn rows); tileable sheets
repeated `tiles` times (256 across a patch = 32-unit tiles, ~1 texel per
unit); an untiled row is one stamp of the *zone-wide* sheet at
`(patch + uv) / 8`; no v flip. Descending order, patch-local stamps and a v
flip all score 1.7–3× worse against that bake. Depth measured over a corpus
sample: at most 9 layers / 3 masks (`notes/layer-span.py`).

The material is built per patch but the program is shared per layer count
(every differing value rides in uniforms), and the renderer's maximum
anisotropy now applies to the ground. `docs/formats.md` §11 carries the
measured model and its one acknowledged difference: the engine rounds to
8 bits between its `PassTwo…PassFour` draws, our single pass does not.

Verified in the browser (harness): 64/64 patches composed on Camelot Hills,
the near-field grain back at the keep (`notes/world-shots/`
`Camelot_Hills-keep-north-layers.png`), the whole zone in
`…-ground-layers.png`; 55 web tests green (7 new, `ground.test.ts`).
